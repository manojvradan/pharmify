# Competitor analysis

Vivascript would not be first: GdayPharmacist already sells AI voice simulation of all three oral exam parts from $299, while human-led courses such as Elite Expertise dominate the premium end. The opening is narrower than "AI oral practice": it is trustworthy, criteria-based grading and exam-faithful Part C practice. This is based on vendor websites, so treat their claims as marketing, and test GdayPharmacist yourself before deciding.

## Adjacent competitors

Outside Australian pharmacy exams, AI voice patients with auto-marked checklists already exist, which shows the approach works and that a funded team could enter this niche.

| Product | What it does | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Pharmacy OSCE Simulator](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pharmacy-osce-simulator/id6755903945) (iOS app) | Live AI voice patient, a generator that turns pasted cases into stations, checklist feedback and English correction; subscription | Built for Canada's PEBC and pharmacy school OSCEs, so it could be adapted to the Australian exam |
| [Geeky Medics virtual patients](https://geekymedics.com/ai-osce-practice/) | 800+ AI virtual patients by text or voice, with instant AI feedback that auto-marks examiner checklists | Medicine-focused, but it proves demand and could add pharmacy |
| [Geeky Medics SimChat](https://geekymedics.com/virtual-patient-simulator/) | Platform for institutions to build and run virtual patient scenarios, with text, voice or video | The most likely competitor for the university channel |
| [Oxford AI-assisted simulated patient](https://www.ctl.ox.ac.uk/simulated-patient-chatbot-for-medical-students-to-practice-communication-and-reasoning) and [CPX-MATE](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.21.26346803.full.pdf) | University and research tools: LLM simulated patients, and a voice platform with automated checklist scoring | Evidence that voice role play with AI marking is workable in assessment settings |
| [General voice assistants](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12175028/) such as ChatGPT voice mode | Free or cheap role play. A study with medical students found value in the feedback and the safe practice, but also technical problems and a limited emotional range | The free substitute candidates may try first; it lacks neutrality, timing and a rubric |

## Where Vivascript can win

Vivascript wins only if it is more trustworthy than GdayPharmacist's AI practice and cheaper or more scalable than human coaching, so the plan should test that directly.

Possible advantages, each unverified until we compare against the competitor:

- **Grading tied to the Board's criteria.** The report is built on the four fail conditions and the Appendix 1 evidence, and quotes the transcript for every judgement.
- **A strict exam mode.** A neutral examiner with no hints, separated from a review mode, as the real exam works.
- **Part C realism.** The prescription and dispensing history shown as documents, a prescriber persona who pushes back, and open-book timing.
- **Visible quality control.** Every scenario is pharmacist-reviewed, versioned and sourced, which a candidate or a provider can check.

Where we are weaker today:

- **Content depth.** GdayPharmacist lists 60 scenarios and Elite claims hundreds, against our planned 10.
- **Human feedback and trust.** Elite has founders who are practising pharmacists and a large review base.
- **Avatar video is not a moat.** GdayPharmacist already offers voice, and video adds cost per minute without adding exam realism.

## What to do about it

1. Buy GdayPharmacist's oral simulation as a candidate and record where it fails on neutrality, realism and grading. Do this before building more.
2. Change the pilot question from "does AI role play work" to "which practice do interns trust more", and test head to head with a few interns.
3. Keep voice-only for the MVP and drop video until the grader is proven.
4. Treat human-led providers as possible partners, since they have the audience but no scalable practice tool, and ask one of them what they would pay for it.
5. Re-scope the scenario target after the test: either match competitors' breadth or pick a narrower niche, such as Part C depth.
